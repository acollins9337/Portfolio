TAPNAV

TAPNAV is a robotics navigation pipeline that allows humanoid robots to feel their way around their environment with strategic contacts. A global planner plans a route with the lowest amount of predicted uncertainty that also minimzies distance. At test time, TAPNAV propogates uncertainty when the robot is moving, then reduces it when contact are made with walls and obstacles. Whole body control is achieved through upper and lower body policies that track end effector pose and foot pose.

We tested TAPNAV on both simulation and real world deployments. One of my contributions was evironment simulation, and ensuring feasibility for navigation. I generated a set of maps that mimicked real world rooms, including curving hallways, tight corridors, and larger more open spaces. I then added objects to these rooms in a pseudo-random fashion, selecting from a set of reasonable points where obstacles might be in real rooms. I included tolerances to ensure that no spaces on the maps were too small for the robot to pass through unless it was intentionally so. Each of these maps had 2D representations for global planning as well as MuJoCo versions for simulation. These maps were used as part of a study to prove that TAPNAV generalized to various maps and not just the cleaner ones tested in some of our real world experiments.

My other major contribution to TAPNAV was working through the implementation of the uncertainty propagation for the global planner. Without the global planner accounting for uncertainty, the modified A* algorithm would simply take the shortest route. Instead, the planner estimates the amount of uncertainty the real robot would gain when in motion, and propogates that at every time step. It then discards any valid routes with too much uncertainty to help prevent the robot from taking potentially dangerous paths.
